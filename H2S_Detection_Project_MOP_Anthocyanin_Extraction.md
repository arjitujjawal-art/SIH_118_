# MOP --- Ethanol-Free Aqueous Extraction of Anthocyanin

## H₂S Detection Project

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Objective

To extract anthocyanin from plant leaves using **only distilled water as
the extraction solvent**, followed by controlled pH adjustment to obtain
a purple anthocyanin solution suitable for further **H₂S-induced
color-change experiments**.

### Target Response

> **Purple anthocyanin solution → H₂S exposure → Red/pink color**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Materials Required

-   Fresh leaves containing anthocyanin
-   Distilled/deionized water
-   Analytical balance
-   Oven/dryer
-   Mortar and pestle or grinder
-   Ultrasonic bath
-   Centrifuge
-   Centrifuge tubes
-   Filter paper
-   Funnel
-   pH meter
-   Dilute NaOH solution
-   Dilute citric acid solution
-   Amber/opaque storage bottle
-   Aluminium foil
-   Ice bath

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Sample Preparation

### Step 3.1 --- Washing

1.  Collect the required quantity of leaves.
2.  Wash the leaves gently with distilled water to remove dust and
    surface contaminants.
3.  Remove excess water using clean tissue/filter paper.

### Step 3.2 --- Drying

1.  Spread the leaves in a single layer.
2.  Dry at approximately **40--45 °C**.
3.  Continue drying until the leaves reach a constant dry mass.
4.  Avoid direct sunlight during drying.
5.  Record the final dry mass.

> **Preferred:** Freeze-drying can be used instead of oven drying if
> available.

### Step 3.3 --- Grinding

1.  Grind the dried leaves into a fine powder.
2.  Minimize exposure to strong light during grinding.
3.  Store the powder in an opaque/amber container until extraction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Aqueous Anthocyanin Extraction

### Extraction Ratio

Use:

**2.00 g dried leaf powder : 50 mL distilled water**

This corresponds to:

**1 g leaf powder : 25 mL water**

### Procedure

1.  Weigh **2.00 g** of dried leaf powder.
2.  Transfer the powder into a clean amber or covered extraction
    container.
3.  Add **50 mL distilled water**.
4.  Mix thoroughly to wet the entire powder.
5.  Place the container in an ultrasonic bath.
6.  Sonicate for approximately **30 minutes**.
7.  Keep the extraction temperature preferably **below 30 °C**. If the
    ultrasonic bath becomes warm, use cooling or intermittent
    sonication.
8.  Protect the extraction mixture from strong light during the process.

### Important

**Do not adjust the extraction mixture to pH 7 at this stage.**

The objective is to extract the anthocyanins first and optimize their
final color afterward.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Separation of Extract

### Centrifugation

After sonication:

1.  Transfer the extraction mixture into centrifuge tubes.
2.  Centrifuge at approximately:

**3500 rpm × 15 minutes**

3.  Carefully collect the supernatant without disturbing the solid
    residue.

### Filtration

1.  Filter the collected supernatant through filter paper.
2.  If available, perform a second filtration using a finer membrane
    filter.
3.  Collect the clear filtrate in an amber container.

The resulting liquid is the:

> **Crude aqueous anthocyanin extract**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. pH Optimization

The crude aqueous extract should **not immediately be adjusted to pH
7**.

Instead, divide the extract into five equal samples.

### Recommended pH Screening

  Sample     Target pH
  -------- -----------
  A                5.0
  B                5.5
  C                6.0
  D                6.5
  E                7.0

### Procedure

1.  Transfer equal volumes of extract into five clean containers.
2.  Measure the initial pH.
3.  Slowly adjust each sample using **dilute NaOH solution**.
4.  Use a calibrated pH meter.
5.  Add the NaOH gradually while continuously mixing.
6.  Avoid adding large quantities at once.
7.  If the pH overshoots, correct it carefully using dilute citric acid.
8.  After reaching the target pH, allow each sample to equilibrate for
    approximately **15--30 minutes in darkness**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Selection of Working Extract

Select the pH condition that provides:

> **Strong purple color + good stability + sufficient color contrast for
> subsequent H₂S exposure**

Do not assume that pH 7 will necessarily be the optimum condition.

If pH 6.0 or 6.5 produces a stronger and more stable purple than pH 7.0,
that condition should be retained for subsequent experiments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. H₂S Color-Change Experiment

The selected anthocyanin solution can then be evaluated for its response
to controlled H₂S exposure.

### Expected Principle

**Initial state**

🟣 **Purple**

↓

**H₂S exposure**

↓

**Change in chemical environment / pH**

↓

🔴 **Red/Pink**

The exact color transition and exposure-response relationship must be
experimentally determined.

### Record for Every Exposure

-   Initial pH
-   Initial RGB/HSV/L\*a\*b\*
-   H₂S exposure condition
-   Exposure duration
-   Final pH
-   Final RGB/HSV/L\*a\*b\*
-   Final color
-   ΔColor

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Storage

The aqueous anthocyanin extract should be:

-   Stored in an **amber/opaque container**
-   Protected from direct light
-   Kept at low temperature where practical
-   Minimized for repeated air exposure
-   Used as soon as reasonably possible

Prepare fresh extract for important experiments rather than relying on
an old extract.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Critical Controls

For reliable results, maintain the following constant:

-   Leaf mass
-   Water volume
-   Leaf-to-water ratio
-   Extraction temperature
-   Sonication time
-   Centrifugation speed
-   Centrifugation time
-   Filtration method
-   pH
-   Sample volume
-   Lighting during color measurement

## 13. Safety

> **H₂S is highly toxic.**

H₂S generation and exposure testing must be performed only in an
appropriate laboratory setup with proper engineering controls, gas
detection, ventilation/fume-hood protection, and institutional safety
procedures.

**Do not perform open-air H₂S bubbling or deliberately expose yourself
to the gas.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Key Modification

The extraction itself is now **100% aqueous --- zero ethanol**.

The pH is optimized **after extraction**, which allows the experiment to
investigate the purple-forming condition without unnecessarily changing
the extraction stage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Project Role

This MOP defines the **anthocyanin extraction and color-response
preparation stage** of the H₂S detection project.

### Core Experimental Chain

**Plant leaves → aqueous anthocyanin extraction → pH optimization →
stable purple indicator → controlled H₂S exposure → measurable color
change → RGB/HSV/L\*a\*b\* analysis**

The resulting color-change response can subsequently be used as the
chemical indicator component of the H₂S detection system.
